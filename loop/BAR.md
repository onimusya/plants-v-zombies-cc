# The Bar — what "winning" means

In blind side-by-side A/B comparison against the equivalent lawn sequence from the
original *Plants vs. Zombies*, our game must win on ALL of these. A critic judges
each piece against this sheet and names the **single biggest remaining gap**.

## Winning criteria (whole game)
1. **Visual charm** — warm, cozy, hand-crafted backyard diorama; expressive, confident,
   personality-rich units; the scene looks like a lovingly built toy game, not a tech demo.
2. **Instant lane readability** — you can read all 5 lanes and every unit's row/lane
   instantly, at a glance, without squinting.
3. **Animation / game-feel** — units bob, sway, lurch, react; nothing is stiff or dead;
   motion sells character and reads clearly.
4. **Combat feedback** — hits, deaths, projectile impacts, plant recovery, sun collection
   all read instantly and feel satisfying (impact pops, flashes, knockback, body language).
5. **Strategic clarity** — you always know what each plant does, what each zombie does, and
   what the right play is. Cost/value, ranges, and threats are legible.
6. **Overall polish** — UI, layout, presentation frame, transitions, and details are
   confident and complete; no placeholder look, no dead zones.

## What makes real PVZ win (calibration)
- Every unit is a tiny character with a face and personality; silhouettes iconically read
  at tiny sizes.
- The environment is a lived-in backyard: house with porch, fence, trees, pond, lawn
  furniture, warm daytime light with gentle shadows.
- Feedback is "juicy": sun pulsates and is satisfying to click, peas have cute arcs,
  zombies stumble comically and die with a satisfying pop + a translucent skinned body.
- The UI frame is charming: seed cards look like paper/seed packets, sun counter is a
  chunky HUD, wave banners are crisp and delightful.
- Everything is readable AND adorable at the same time.
